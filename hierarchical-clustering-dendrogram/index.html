Agglomerative Hierarchical Clustering

Agglomerative (bottom-up) hierarchical clustering starts with every point as its own cluster. At each step, the two closest clusters are merged, until only one cluster remains. The sequence of merges forms a binary tree called a dendrogram: each merge is drawn as a U-shaped bracket whose height equals the distance at which the merge happened. How "closeness" between two clusters \(A\) and \(B\) is measured is called the linkage:

\[\text{single:}\quad d(A,B) = \min_{a \in A,\, b \in B} \lVert a - b \rVert \qquad\qquad \text{complete:}\quad d(A,B) = \max_{a \in A,\, b \in B} \lVert a - b \rVert\] \[\text{average:}\quad d(A,B) = \frac{1}{|A||B|} \sum_{a \in A} \sum_{b \in B} \lVert a - b \rVert \qquad\qquad \text{centroid:}\quad d(A,B) = \lVert \mu_A - \mu_B \rVert\]
Watch the left panel to see clusters being merged (the dashed line shows the pair of clusters chosen and the distance defining their linkage), and the right panel to see the dendrogram grow with each merge. To get a flat clustering of \(k\) clusters, cut the dendrogram at some height: use the cut slider and see the scatter plot recolor instantly. Single linkage tends to produce chained, stringy merges; complete linkage produces compact balanced ones. (Note: centroid linkage can produce inversions — a merge lower than a previous one.)
